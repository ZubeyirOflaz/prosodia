# Editor's notes on episode 2 — NOT marked ready

The writer/editor loop ended at its round limit rather than on a clean verdict, so the defects below are still in `transcript.md`.

## BLOCKING

**1. An episode/forward pointer inside the first five minutes (the `error` line).** The count is one reference, and it lands early, which is the one placement this series treats as unrecoverable — a listener two minutes in is handed a debt they cannot discharge.

The sentence I read as the offender is in the cold open, closing the Lessig paragraph:

> "Lessig's reply went to what a new subject makes visible that an old one hides. **I will come back to that, because it turns out to be the most useful idea in this episode.**"

That plants a pointer at roughly the two-minute mark and does not redeem it until "Lessig's answer, and the idea underneath it" — about eight minutes later. Cut the second sentence entirely. Nothing is lost: "Lessig's reply went to what a new subject makes visible that an old one hides" is a complete, satisfying beat on its own, and the Lessig section re-introduces itself cleanly ("Lessig replied in 1999") without needing to have been promised.

The other candidate in the same window, if the count caught this one instead, is the Episode 1 callback:

> "This is The Instrument. **The gate is built:** what makes a thing an AI system at all, and the moment anybody's duty begins."

The content after the colon is correct and self-sufficient — it restates the prerequisite rather than pointing at it. The fix is to drop the framing that it happened elsewhere: "This is The Instrument. What makes a thing an AI system at all, and the moment anybody's duty begins — that is built." Apply both fixes and the count is clean either way; both are cheap and both improve the opening.

Nothing else blocks. The source check, the lens spine and the comprehension counts all pass — details under CLEARED so the next round does not re-run them.

## IMPROVEMENTS

**The four-item run, and why it is only an improvement.** The flagged sentence is:

> "There is no clause, no author on the record, no exception procedure, and nobody to petition, because from inside the system it does not present as a decision at all."

Four items, over the persona's three. I am not blocking it: nothing downstream asks the listener to retain the members, and the very next line compresses the whole thing into three named properties — "The constraint is total, it is invisible, and it was never argued about." That restatement does the work the rule exists to protect. Still, cut to three and it costs nothing: "There is no clause, no author on the record, and nobody to petition." The preceding positive list ("A legal rule has a text you can read. It has an author you can name. It usually has an exception, and it always has a route by which somebody can challenge it") is also four, spread over four clauses; trim "It usually has an exception" and the mirror stays intact at three-against-three.

**4.5 minutes with no breath.** The longest unbroken stretch in the episode starts at "Lessig's answer, and the idea underneath it" and runs 584 words to the Collingridge retrieval beat — and it carries the episode's densest new idea. Every paragraph in it lands before the next begins, which is why this is not blocking, but two insertions would buy a lot: a `{pause: 1.4}` after "The constraint is total, it is invisible, and it was never argued about," and another after "Just not by anyone you elected."

**"Use one of them, and you were in" claims more than the docket holds.** `08_lens_sources.md` establishes that COM(2021) 206 "defined an AI system by reference to techniques and approaches listed in its Annex I." It does not hold the proposal's definitional sentence, and that sentence made the technique list *necessary*, not sufficient — there were further conditions about objectives and outputs. "Use one of them, and you were in" asserts sufficiency the docket cannot support. The contrast the episode actually needs — a list of techniques, against a test of inference — survives intact if you write "The list was the way in." Change four words and the beat is unimpeached.

**"Seven months, a full legislative round, to change a date."** Accurate as to what you measure (proposed November 2025, Parliament approved June 2026 — both in docket 00), and the argument turns on *which mechanism* moved the date, which is right. But Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 did considerably more than move dates: two new prohibitions, and Articles 4a, 60a and 75a–75d. A listener will leave thinking it was a date-moving instrument. Half a clause fixes it and belongs to no other episode: "a full legislative round — which did other things too, but this is the one that matters here."

**The Downes quotation needs an audible closing bracket.** You open it properly — "His sentence, and these are his words:" — and then the listener has no signal where he stops. "That is the sentence the field has been arguing with ever since" half-closes it; make it explicit: "Those are his words. Mine now: it does more than it looks." Separately, `08_lens_sources.md` verifies the formulation but carries "⚠ confirm the page before quoting it as his words." No page is spoken, so this is not a defect, but the check is worth doing before record.

**Render the symbols for the ear.** The episode spells every date out in words — "the twenty-first of April 2021", "the thirtieth of November 2022", "the second of December 2027" — and then leaves two items in written form that a narrator or engine must guess at: **"COM(2021) 206 final"** and **"Code: Version 2.0"**. Write them as they should sound. The citation discipline itself is right: given once, then replaced by "the April 2021 text" and "the proposal", exactly as required.

**"For stand-alone systems" is unglossed.** The distinction it carries (Annex III against systems embedded in already-regulated products) is nowhere in this episode and belongs elsewhere. Either drop the qualifier or spend four words: "for systems sold on their own".

**Three-beat crescendos, which the series rules put off-limits.** At least three: "They apply to everyone. They enforce themselves. Nobody voted for them." · "No gate. No tiers. No duty on anything at all until it has hurt somebody." · "There are jobs. There is sunk capital. Everyone holding either will fight you." Each is effective in isolation; together they become the episode's default shape for a landing. Break one or two into a different rhythm.

**One instance of the banned sentence shape.** "That does not mean Easterbrook was wrong about the cost; it means the cost was paid." It is doing genuine analytic work, so it is the forgivable instance — but it is the "not X, it's Y" construction the series rules bar, and it is the only one, so it is free to lose.

**The handover blurs a jurisdiction by one word.** "An airline once argued in a tribunal for a third option **the law** does not contain." Read forward from "The Act has an answer", "the law" scans as the Act — which did not govern Air Canada. "a third option no such law contains" keeps the tease and makes no claim Episode 3 then has to walk back.

**Rhythm has drifted short, not long.** Median 10 against a target of 12, with 42% at eight words or fewer against a third. That is the safe direction to drift, and the staccato is deliberate in places (Easterbrook's three defects; the Collingridge trap). But the Collingridge section in particular could absorb two longer connective sentences without losing its snap.

**The Reidenberg gloss goes a little past the source.** "They apply to everyone. They enforce themselves. Nobody voted for them" is a fair unpacking of "technical capability sets rules that behave like law", but `08_lens_sources.md` warns that its summaries are the compiler's and nothing in it was read in full. Either soften to the narrator's voice ("which is to say — and this is me drawing it out —") or read the article.
