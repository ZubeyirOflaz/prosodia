## The thesis, from its author

**Anu Bradford, *The Brussels Effect: How the European Union Rules the World* (New York: Oxford
University Press, 2020), ISBN 9780190088583, ~404 pp.** *Verified: author, exact title, publisher,
year, ISBN. Bradford is a professor at Columbia Law School.* This closes the plan's
`[OUTSIDE DOCKET]` marker — the book may now be cited by name, publisher and year.

**Two mechanisms, and keep them apart — the episode's whole argument turns on the distinction:**

- **De facto** — a firm adjusts its *global* operations to the EU rule because market incentives
  make one worldwide product cheaper than two. No foreign government does anything.
- **De jure** — a foreign legislature *copies* the rule. Bradford's account is that this often
  follows the de facto effect: once multinationals have already adapted, they acquire an interest
  in lobbying for the same rule at home, which removes their competitive disadvantage.

**Five cumulative conditions.** Market size · regulatory capacity · stringent standards ·
inelastic targets · **non-divisibility**. *Cumulative* is doing work: on Bradford's own account the
effect needs all five, so failing one is enough to break it.

> **The analytic hinge for THIS series, and the reason the episode is worth its minutes.**
> Non-divisibility is the condition that decides the AI Act's case, and it is the one most open to
> doubt. A phone has one port. A model can be served with different behaviour, different
> guardrails and different documentation per region, at a cost that may be far below the cost of
> running two supply chains. **If AI systems are more divisible than physical goods, Bradford's
> own framework predicts a weaker Brussels effect here than for chemicals or privacy** — and the
> episode can reach that conclusion using her conditions rather than against them.

## For, applied to AI

**Charlotte Siegmann and Markus Anderljung, "The Brussels Effect and Artificial Intelligence: How
EU regulation will impact the global AI market", Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI),
arXiv:2208.12645, 23 August 2022.** *Verified: both authors, exact title, institution, arXiv
number, submission date. The plan's note that the docket "gives the institution but not the
authors' names" is now closed — attribute to Siegmann and Anderljung.*

Both de facto and de jure effects likely **for parts** of the regime, with a de facto effect
particularly likely in **large US technology companies operating systems the Act classes as
high-risk**. Note the hedge is theirs: *parts*, not the whole.

## Against

**Anda Bologa, "Burying the Brussels Effect? AI Act Inspires Few Copycats", Center for European
Policy Analysis (CEPA), 23 April 2025.** *Verified: author, exact title, institution, date.*

Counts the copycats and finds almost none: **Canada and Brazil** drafting comparable frameworks,
both stalled in legislative limbo; **Peru** with two AI laws echoing the EU template and seventeen
further bills, but — the piece's own caveat — without the oversight capacity to make them bite.

> **This is a disagreement about the DE JURE effect only, and the episode must say so.** Bologa
> counts statutes. Siegmann and Anderljung predict firm behaviour. Two teams measuring different
> mechanisms is not the same as two teams disagreeing, and treating it as a head-to-head would be
> the easy, wrong version of this episode.

**A reconceptualisation, if a second critical voice is wanted:** Ylönen, "Reconceptualising the
Brussels Effect", *JCMS: Journal of Common Market Studies* (2026), DOI `10.1111/jcms.13731`.

## The de facto mechanism, with an actual instance

The plan records this as the missing item. Here it is, and it is unusually clean because the firm
**stated its own reason in public**, so causation does not have to be inferred.

**Microsoft, May 2018.** **Julie Brill**, corporate vice-president and deputy general counsel,
announced on Microsoft's official blog that the company would *"extend the rights that are at the
heart of GDPR to all of our consumer customers worldwide"* — naming the right to know what data is
collected, to correct it, to delete it and to take it elsewhere. Her stated reasoning: *"As an EU
regulation, GDPR creates important new rights specifically for individuals in the European Union.
But we believe GDPR establishes important principles that are relevant globally."*
*Verified: company, named officer and title, month and year, and the quoted wording, from
Microsoft's own "On the Issues" blog, 21 May 2018.*

**And it was used, which is the harder half.** Microsoft reported in **September 2018** that
millions had used the new privacy tools, **including about two million people in the United
States** — a population the GDPR does not reach. *Verified: Microsoft "On the Issues",
17 September 2018.* ⚠ *Treat "about two million" as the company's own figure, reported by the
company, and say so.*

**Why this instance and not a better-known one.** It is first-party, dated, attributed to a named
officer, and followed by evidence of uptake outside the EU. Most Brussels-effect examples are
inferred from a firm's conduct; this one is the firm explaining itself.

**A second instance, with its weakness stated.** **Directive (EU) 2022/2380** (the common charger)
required USB-C on phones sold in the EU from 28 December 2024, and Apple shipped USB-C on the
iPhone 15 **worldwide** in September 2023. ⚠ **Causation is arguable and the episode must not
overstate it**: the directive applies to devices sold in the EU, Apple never stated the EU rule as
its reason, and there were independent technical reasons to move. Use it as an illustration of
**non-divisibility** — one port, one product, so the strictest rule wins everywhere — not as proof
of intent.

## What this does not give you

- **No instance in AI specifically.** Every documented de facto case here is privacy or hardware.
  That is not a gap in the research; it is the state of the world in 2026, and it is the episode's
  most honest finding: **the mechanism is well evidenced, its application to AI is a prediction.**
  Say that plainly rather than reaching for an AI example that does not exist.
- **No counting of de jure adoption since April 2025.** Bologa's count is the most recent in this
  docket; if the episode states a number of copycat statutes it should date-stamp it to that piece.
