# Editor's notes on episode 10 — NOT marked ready

The writer/editor loop ended at its round limit rather than on a clean verdict, so the defects below are still in `transcript.md`.

BLOCKING

1. The third deciding fact gets the data half of the Omnibus wrong. This contradicts docket 04 Part Two and docket 06 Part Four. In "Where I come down":
"And on everything this programme holds, the package that would amend the data protection Regulation adds two things. It adds a basis for processing, and a right to object. It adds no route to compensation."
The programme holds more than two things. Docket 04 lists two proposals. One is the Art. 88c legitimate-interest route, which comes with enhanced transparency and an unconditional right to object. The other is "an exemption for residual special-category data that finds its way into a training set". Docket 06 repeats the residual exemption and reports consent provisions as well. "Adds two things" is a closed count, and the opening words "on everything this programme holds" make it a claim about the docket. The docket says otherwise. The item left out is also the one that touches the prohibition, and two later passages depend on it. The overturn condition says "Suppose its final text keeps the prohibition intact". The closing question says "ask whether the prohibition is still in it". Both assume the slow half could change the prohibition, but nothing in this episode says so. A listener new to the series hears a proposal that adds a basis and a right to object. Minutes later they are asked whether it kept a prohibition they were never told it touches. Replace with:
"And on everything this programme holds, the package that would amend the data protection Regulation adds a basis for processing, with a right to object attached. It adds an exemption for protected data that ends up in a training set anyway. It adds no route to compensation."
Recommended but not blocking: earn this in the opening too. In "Two halves", after "The proposed new route for building AI on legitimate interests is still only a proposal.", add "So is an exemption for protected data that ends up in a training set."

IMPROVEMENTS

1. Two answers are referred to by number alone, late in the episode: "The fourth answer is how to see the verdict. The second answer is what it costs." This comes twenty minutes after the answers were numbered, so a listener who drifted cannot tell which is which. Change to: "The fourth answer, different addressees, is how to see the verdict. The second, duties multiplied and the same gap left, is what it costs." This was the one failure in the three-point rejoin test. The other two points passed.
2. "That later ruling, from 2025, is the nearest the record comes to one." A ruling is not an account addressed to CK. The lens episode also said the Court answered a question about access, not about reasons. Change to "the nearest the record comes to requiring one."
3. "Where a lender leans heavily on the score, the duties move upstream." "Move" can be heard as releasing the bank. The holding puts the duties on the scorer, not only on the lender. Use "reach upstream". In the same way, "It put the duties … onto the company" could become "It reached past the bank to the company".
4. "Then the Court in Luxembourg reached past a bank." Coming straight after August 2024, "Then" sounds chronological, but SCHUFA was decided in December 2023. Use "And the Court in Luxembourg had already reached past a bank."
5. In the fourth answer: "And the damage list was built around bodies and property." The list has a third entry, data not used for work, and the script gives it later. Say "built mostly around bodies and property", which matches the wording in the close.
6. The opening has six dates in about ninety seconds. Each is tied to an event, so this is not a list of numbers, but the 29 June and 27 July pair could be one phrase: "It became law at the end of June, and has applied since late July". Keep 5 October and the beginning of 2027 exact.
7. Rhythm: the median sentence is 10 words against the persona's 12. The sentence-splitting has gone too far and turned staccato in places, worst in the first answer ("It wanted to stop a platform using its users' posts to train a model. It used a data protection power. And it got there…") and in the opening. Rejoin a handful of sentences there, and don't split any more.
8. Small: two pieces of the instruments' own wording are spoken without audible brackets. They are "may, exceptionally and where strictly necessary, process" (Art. 4a's own "may exceptionally process") and "material or non-material damage" (Art. 82). Either bracket them or paraphrase them further.
9. Length: 4,337 words, about 33 minutes against 30. That is within the brief's licence and the writer flagged it. Nothing needs cutting.
