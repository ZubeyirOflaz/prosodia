# Editor's notes on episode 2 — NOT marked ready

The writer/editor loop ended at its round limit rather than on a clean verdict, so the defects below are still in `transcript.md`.

## BLOCKING

**1. The compatibility test is described wrongly, in the sentence that carries the episode's thesis.**

> "One more thing about the compatibility test, and it is the thing the rest of the episode turns on. Every factor in it compares a new purpose with an old one. So the test cannot even start until somebody can say what the new purpose *is*."

Three of the five factors in Article 6(4) do not compare purposes: the context of collection and the relationship between subject and controller (backward-looking only), the nature of the data, and the existence of safeguards such as encryption or pseudonymisation. The script contradicts itself three paragraphs earlier, where it correctly says of that same factor: "That factor asks what the relationship actually was, when you typed it." A listener who took this away would look for comparisons that are not there, and would miss that safeguards — factor five — can carry an otherwise incompatible further use, which is directly material to a training corpus.

The conclusion is right and there is a stronger premise for it in the chapeau. Replace with: "The test only exists at all where data is being processed *for a purpose other than the one it was collected for*. Those are the opening words of the provision. So the test cannot even start until somebody can say what the new purpose *is*."

**2. An inference is drawn that contradicts the docket, on the episode's central contested question, with the other side's named holder omitted.**

> "Contested: whether legitimate interests can carry it. The fact that a legislative amendment is being negotiated to say so expressly tells you it does not say so now."

Docket 04 records the EDPB and the EDPS saying the opposite of that inference — that a specific legitimate-interest provision for AI training was not necessary at all, "since legitimate interests may already serve in some cases." And docket 03 records that the second and third of Opinion 28/2024's four questions are *how a controller demonstrates legitimate interest* in development and in deployment — the Board answered how, not whether. The episode already has that Opinion on stage and uses its fourth question at length, so the material is in hand.

As written, a listener comes away believing the current text clearly does not permit this basis. That is an apparatus mis-taught. Cut the inference sentence and put the named holder in its place: "Contested — and both sides have names. The same Board, in the same Opinion, was asked how a controller demonstrates legitimate interest for the development phase, and answered the question rather than closing the door. And when a provision was proposed to say so expressly, the Board and the European Data Protection Supervisor said it was not needed, because legitimate interests may already serve. So: conditionally available on the regulator's own view, untested by any court this programme has found."

**3. The load-bearing claim lives only in the last quarter of a thirty-nine-minute episode and is never returned to.**

The plan names the claim that must land: this Regulation asks what the data is for, and the answer that makes the model valuable is "we don't know yet." It appears as a one-sentence plant at roughly minute twenty-four ("in a few minutes I am going to show you a kind of system that cannot supply it"), then in the machine beat at minute thirty, and then nowhere. Nothing in the first twenty minutes states it, and the close does not carry it. A listener who lapses across that one stretch loses the episode's thesis.

Two insertions fix it. First, in "Where we are", after:

> "That is what this episode is about: the reason."

add: "And the trouble waiting at the end of it is that this Regulation wants a purpose named in advance, while the thing being built is valuable precisely because nobody can say yet what it will be used for."

Second, in "What you now ask", after:

> "What was declared, at the moment of collection, to the person who typed it."

add: "Ask that of a general-purpose model and the honest answer is a shrug — and the shrug is the whole problem. This Regulation asks what the data is for. The answer that makes the model worth building is that nobody knows yet."
