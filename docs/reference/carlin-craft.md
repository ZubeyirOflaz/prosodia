# Carlin craft reference

Distilled from six independent analyses of Dan Carlin's **"Supernova in the East"
I–VI"** (Hardcore History). This is the durable gold-standard the refinement loops
grade against — so we never have to re-ingest the transcripts. Split by which role
owns each technique: the **Planner** (series/plan structure) vs. the **Writer**
(the verbatim prose the narrator speaks). See also [`../pipeline-and-traces.md`](../pipeline-and-traces.md).

> Sourcing caveat: the public transcripts truncated at 35–75%, so *closing*
> techniques are partly inferred; openings, scope, throughline, immersion, and
> historiography are directly evidenced across all six episodes.

---

## A. Plan-level craft (owned by the PLANNER)

1. **Driving question.** One moral/analytical *question* spans the series and each
   episode is a fresh variation on it ("When do bravery and tenacity cross into
   madness?"). Events are *evidence for the question*, never a checklist.
2. **Depth over breadth / dwell budget.** An episode may cover only weeks of a
   multi-decade story, inhabiting 1–3 set-pieces and compressing the rest to
   sentences (a single massacre can fill 45 min while a year of war passes in two
   lines). Radically uneven pacing is a *virtue*. Cover **less, deeper**.
3. **Finale narrows and escalates.** The last episode tightens scope and raises
   intensity ("opera building toward apocalypse") — it is **not** a coverage dump
   of everything left over. Any topic appearing for the *first* time in the finale
   is a failure; a finale only *re-tests* threads planted earlier.
4. **Scope realism.** Little real-world time per episode; the episode COUNT should
   serve depth, not be assumed.
5. **Seams.** Episodes open on a scene/paradox/question (never "in this episode we
   cover…"), close on suspended tension, and recap is a *thin re-immersion into the
   stakes*, not a summary. Each episode plants threads and pays off earlier ones.
6. **Coverage honesty.** No gaps/overlap **and** no bundling loophole (a topic row
   that silently hides 2–3 episodes of material). Omission is a first-class,
   *stated* choice, not a silent gap.
7. **Texture allocation.** Which episodes carry a tracked human anchor vs. sustained
   source-wrestling is distributed *unevenly* by design — the plan allocates the
   slots even though the prose fills them.

## B. Prose-level craft (owned by the WRITER)

1. **Immersion.** Second person ("imagine you are a Japanese commander…"), sensory
   scene-setting, explicit counterfactuals ("what if the vote had gone the other
   way?"), sustained rhetorical questions.
2. **Voice / register.** One narrator carrying long stretches; direct address; wry
   register-breaks and modern analogies to reset intensity ("imperialism as
   steroids"); scaffolding phrases — "hold that thought," "more on that later,"
   "remember…" — that manage a multi-hour arc.
3. **Dwelling in prose.** Spend real words inhabiting the set-pieces; compress
   connective tissue to a sentence; *narrate the pacing choice aloud* ("let me pause
   here"; "I find it more interesting to focus on the soldiers than the maps").
4. **Human anchors, executed.** Track a *specific named* individual through a
   passage (Onoda, John Rabe, Eugene Sledge, a single mother) so one life carries
   the macro theme.
5. **Source-wrestling in prose.** Name historians and quote them; weigh conflicting
   accounts and give the *range* ("the death toll differs source to source"); admit
   your own limits ("take this with a grain of salt"); warn against presentism
   ("they didn't know how it turns out"). The doubt is *in* the narration by design.
6. **Moral stakes, realized.** Build to a real, often unresolved human/ethical
   question and make the listener *feel its weight* ("would we stop Holocaust #2
   tomorrow?") — distinct from tone.
7. **Opening hook + closing beat, in prose.** Cold-open on a concrete scene/anomaly
   ("a young soldier is dropped on a jungle island… what accounts for that?"); end
   on suspended tension, not a tidy wrap.
8. **Thematic tangents.** Signposted digressions and comparanda (Assyrians → My Lai
   → the present day) that prove the throughline is universal — each returns to the
   spine; never a random detour.
9. **Analogy for scale.** Relatable analogies to make numbers/abstractions land
   (a stadium; the spider in your hotel room).
10. **Emotional dynamic range.** Horror unsanitized; humor as relief; a deliberate
    rhythm of strategy ↔ ground-level human story.

## How the loops use this

- The **planner refinement loop** grades a series outline against §A.
- The **writer refinement loop** grades a written transcript sample against §B.
- Anything a reviewer wants to "fix" that maps to §B is **not** the planner's to fix
  (route it to the writer), and vice versa — this is the root-cause tagging the loops
  rely on.
